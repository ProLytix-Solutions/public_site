The Problem

Manual classification
doesn't scale, and errors compound.

Business license and tax classification is still largely a manual process in many revenue offices — a clerk reading a business description, matching it against a rate table by hand, and applying minimums, maximums, and multipliers that vary by jurisdiction. It's slow, it's inconsistent between staff, and the reasoning behind a given figure is hard to reconstruct on audit if it wasn't written down at the time.

What The Platform Does
  • Classifies business descriptions against jurisdiction-specific tax and license tables
  • Calculates fees with minimums, maximums, and rate multipliers applied automatically
  • Shows the step-by-step reasoning trace behind every classification and calculation
  • Falls back to keyword matching when network access isn't available
  • A revenue officer reviews and confirms every result — the platform proposes, it does not finalize
How It Works

Read the description.
Show the reasoning.

A staff member enters a business description. The platform matches it against the applicable tax and license tables, applies the correct rate multipliers for that jurisdiction, and returns a calculated fee — along with a plain-language explanation of every step that produced it. The reviewer sees the reasoning, not just the number.

Classification

Business descriptions matched against tax and license tables using guided reasoning, not a black-box model. Every match cites the category it used.

Calculation

Minimum and maximum bounds and jurisdiction-specific multipliers applied automatically, with the arithmetic shown at every step.

Audit Trail

Every classification and calculation logged with the reasoning that produced it — defensible on audit, consistent across staff and over time.
